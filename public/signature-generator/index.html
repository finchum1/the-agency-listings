Add it to your email app

Pick your email app. In every case: click Copy signature above first, then follow the steps.

  1. In Gmail, click the gear (top right), then See all settings.
  2. On the General tab, scroll down to Signature and click Create new. Give it a name.
  3. Click inside the signature box and paste (Ctrl+V on Windows, Cmd+V on Mac). Gmail has no HTML box, so paste the signature itself, not the code.
  4. Under Signature defaults, choose this signature for New emails and On reply/forward.
  5. Scroll to the bottom and click Save Changes.
Gmail often shows the logo still while you edit. Send yourself a test email to see it move.
  1. In Outlook on the web or the new Outlook, click the gear (top right), then Accounts → Signatures. (If you see Mail → Compose and reply instead, the signature box is there.)
  2. Click New signature and give it a name.
  3. Paste into the box (Ctrl+V / Cmd+V).
  4. Choose it as the default for new messages and for replies/forwards, then click Save.
  1. In Outlook for Windows, click File → Options → Mail → Signatures…
  2. Click New, name it, and click OK.
  3. Click in the large editing box and paste (Ctrl+V).
  4. Next to New messages and Replies/forwards, choose this signature, then click OK.
Outlook for Windows doesn’t play animated images. It shows the finished logo instead, which still looks right. People opening your email in Gmail, Apple Mail or Outlook on the web will see it animate.
  1. In Outlook for Mac, click Outlook (menu bar) → Settings → Signatures.
  2. Click + to add a signature, then click in the right-hand box.
  3. Paste (Cmd+V).
  4. Set it as the default for new messages and replies, then close the window to save.
  1. In Apple Mail, click Mail (menu bar) → Settings → Signatures.
  2. Click +, then untick “Always match my default message font”. If you skip this, Mail can strip the formatting.
  3. Click in the right-hand box and paste (Cmd+V).
  4. Drag the signature onto your email account in the left column, then choose it in Choose Signature.
If the logo shows as an empty box, send yourself a test first. Apple Mail sometimes needs a moment to fetch the picture.
